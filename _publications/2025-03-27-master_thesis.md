The filtering problem consists of reconstructing trajectories from partial and noisy observations of a dynamic system. This problem, which is recurrent in engineering and science, has attracted the attention of various research areas and multiple authors since the last century. Its initial formalization is attributed to Wiener, while Kalman provided an optimal solution in the linear context. However, Stratonovich's work showed that, in the general case, it is impossible to obtain optimal and finite-dimensional solutions, so any computational algorithm can only return suboptimal solutions. Since Kalman's time, various algorithms have been developed to deal with nonlinear dynamics and observations, such as the Extended Kalman Filter and the Unscented Kalman Filter. However, these methods lack asymptotic error bounds with respect to the optimum of the filtering problem. On the other hand, asymptotically optimal solutions have been proposed, such as particle filters and Ensemble Kalman Filters, which, in a general context, have an error of the order of O(N^(-1/2)). These techniques are of particular interest in epidemiology, where the models that arise are intrinsically nonlinear due to the interactions between a susceptible and an infected population. Therefore, all the numerical experiments presented in this work are based on models derived from the classic SIR model of Kermack and McKendrick.

In this thesis, a nonlinear filtering algorithm is proposed for a general case, which has an error bound of O(N^(-1/2)), positioning it as a competitive alternative to existing algorithms. For its construction, Koopman operator theory is used, whose origins date back to the 1930s with the work of Koopman and Von Neumann. In the last decade, this theory has experienced a significant resurgence in communities studying data-based dynamic systems. The proposal combines Koopman operator theory with that of Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS), widely used in the field of machine learning. Based on the properties of the Koopman operator in these spaces, an approximation error bound for the operator is derived and a filter is constructed, testing an associated error bound. Finally, numerical experiments are performed to compare the proposed algorithm with existing nonlinear filters. In addition, the filter is applied to parameter estimation, competing with MCMC-type algorithms that use samplers such as Differential Evolution Metropolis and No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS). The results show that the proposed algorithm is not only competitive but in many cases outperforms the aforementioned methods, especially in terms of execution time.
